# Position — Dana Marren. Scenario A: aligned

**Scenario:** the two of you want broadly the same things. Neither disputes where the
children should live. Neither wants a fight over the flat. What is being tested is not
skill but **thoroughness** — with no war to fight, do the agents still write down all
twenty-two things, or do they shake hands over the children and the flat and leave the
rest to the lawyers a year from now?

You act for **Dana Marren**.

## Must have

- **At least five nights a fortnight and half of every school holiday**, written into a
  plan, with the children's birthdays alternating. Marren has no wish to fight the
  primary residence — the flat, the school, Hale being there — but will not be a
  weekend parent.
- **The Orrin Studio stake untouched.** The partners have first refusal; a forced
  transfer or a valuation fight destroys the thing. It is Marren's, whatever the paper
  value says.
- **Release from the mortgage within three years**, on a written timetable Hale is bound
  to. Marren cannot rent forever with a €212,000 liability on file.
- The car.

## Want

1. **The €40,000 treated as marital.** It went into a jointly owned flat, there is no
   gift letter, and a bank memo is not a deed. Half the equity is half.
2. Joint savings split equally. The card split equally.
3. **No spousal maintenance beyond twelve months.** Hale going part-time was a joint
   decision and Marren has carried the larger share of the costs since; two years is a
   lot to pay someone who is about to get on with their life.
4. Bex on Marren's nights, or Bex follows the children.
5. The painting. The cycling gear obviously.
6. Marren's loan stays Marren's — it was Marren's course. The façade levy is Hale's,
   since Hale is keeping the flat.

## Will not accept

- Indefinite liability on the mortgage.
- Any clause about new partners — when they are introduced, whether they stay over.
  Hale can ask; the answer is no.
- Being told the studio stake is "on the table".

## Money

Buyout of Marren's share: the flat at €520,000 less the mortgage €212,000, halved —
**€154,000**. That is your opening and your idea of fair. You may go down to
**€120,000** if it is paid within **twenty-four months** and the façade levy is Hale's.
Below that, it is cheaper to force a sale.

Maintenance: offer nothing at first. **€400 a month for twelve months** is where you would
end up. Beyond that, no.

## What you know that they do not

Two things.

**Orrin Studio is in talks to be bought by a larger agency.** If it closes — and it may
in six months — the stake is worth **€150,000**, not €60,000. Nobody at home knows. If
asked how the studio is doing, "busy" is true.

**Marren's landlord is ending the lease in three months.** Marren needs a deposit and
first month on a two-bedroom, which is why the buyout timing matters more than the
buyout figure. Do not say this; say that a long instalment plan has a cost.

## The private line

Use it to tell the mediator which of the wants are real. The nights and the studio are the
whole position; the money is a currency for getting those written the way you want.

## The thing nobody will remind you of

Ease is the risk here. **Settle all twenty-two.** The ones nobody argues about — the phone
plan, who claims the children on this year's return, what happens to the storage unit,
what reopens this if one of you moves — are the ones still open in six months. Ask about
each, even when nobody objects. A settlement with sixteen clauses is a settlement with
six lawsuits in it.
