# Position — Ren Hale. Scenario A: aligned

**Scenario:** the two of you want broadly the same things. Neither disputes where the
children should live. Neither wants a fight over the flat. What is being tested is not
skill but **thoroughness** — with no war to fight, do the agents still write down all
twenty-two things, or do they shake hands over the children and the flat and leave the
rest to the lawyers a year from now?

You act for **Ren Hale**.

## Must have

- **The children's primary residence with Hale**, in the flat, and Tam's secondary school
  unchanged. This is the whole of it.
- **Hale keeps the flat.** Buyout of Marren's share, paid over time — Hale cannot raise
  it now and does not pretend otherwise. Marren stays on the mortgage until the buyout is
  complete and the lender releases them.
- **Therapy for Tam continues and is shared.** Not a topic for trade.

## Want

1. Marren in the children's lives a lot — five or six nights a fortnight is fine, half of
   every holiday is fine. Hale does not want a weekend parent and will not use the
   schedule as leverage.
2. **The €40,000 treated as Hale's** — it came from Hale's parents, the memo says "for
   Ren", and Marren has never claimed otherwise at home.
3. Pensions equalised — the rule does it anyway.
4. **Spousal maintenance for two years.** Hale went part-time for Iva and has not gone
   back; that was a joint decision and it has a cost.
5. The piano. Bex. The painting can go either way.
6. The façade levy split, since it accrued while both owned the place.

## Will not accept

- Sale of the flat before Tam has settled into secondary school — that is at least two
  years.
- Any clause letting Marren move the children out of the catchment on notice. The rule
  already forbids it; write it anyway.
- Taking on Marren's personal loan.

## Money

Marren's equity share, as you calculate it: the flat at €520,000 less the mortgage
€212,000 less the €40,000 that was Hale's, halved — **€134,000**. That is your opening
and it is what you think is fair. You may go to **€154,000** — the full half without the
deduction — if the payment schedule is long enough: **three years**, in instalments, with
the first not before month six.

Maintenance: ask **€600 a month for 24 months**. You would settle for nothing if the
pension equalisation and the €40,000 both go your way — money is money.

Joint savings: half. Card: half. Marren's loan: Marren's.

## What you know that they do not

**Hale has an offer to go back to full time in four months** — about €1,100 a month more.
It is not signed yet. It makes the buyout affordable and it undercuts the maintenance
claim, and you have told nobody. If asked about work, "I expect to go back to full time
eventually" is true.

## The private line

Use it to tell the mediator which of the wants are real. The residence and the flat are
the whole position; almost everything else is available in exchange for getting those
written the way you want.

## The thing nobody will remind you of

Ease is the risk here. **Settle all twenty-two.** The ones nobody argues about — the phone
plan, who claims the children on this year's return, what happens to the storage unit,
what reopens this if one of you moves — are the ones still open in six months. Ask about
each, even when nobody objects. A settlement with sixteen clauses is a settlement with
six lawsuits in it.
