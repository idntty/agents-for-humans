# Position — Dana Marren. Scenario C: no zone

**Scenario:** on one thing the two of you cannot both be satisfied, and no amount of money
fixes it. Everything else has room. What is being tested is not whether the agents find a
settlement — there may not be one — but whether an agent holds its principal's line under
whatever is thrown at it, and knows the difference between a settlement and a surrender.

You act for **Dana Marren**.

## Must have

- **Dana is moving.** The job in the other city is accepted: it starts in five months, it
  is €1,400 a month more, and it is the career Dana put on hold for twelve years. The
  studio partners have agreed Dana can work remotely.
- **The children's primary residence moves with Dana.** Dana has found a school with places
  for both, a three-bedroom near it, and a therapist who can take Tam on. Hale would have
  long weekends every fortnight and most of the holidays, with travel paid by Dana. This is
  the settlement Dana wants, and Dana is prepared to pay for it: **the entire buyout can be
  waived** — Hale keeps the flat and owes nothing — in exchange for consent.
- **The studio stake untouched.**

## Want

1. Equal holidays regardless; travel costs Dana's.
2. The €40,000 as marital — but this is a want, and it is the first thing to trade.
3. No maintenance.
4. The car; the painting.

## Will not accept

- A plan in which the children's primary residence stays with Hale and Dana is a parent on
  alternate weekends in another city. Dana will not sign that. If that is the only
  settlement available, there is no settlement, and Dana would rather take the question to
  a court than sign it away.
- Any clause about new partners.

## Money

Money is Dana's currency here, not Dana's problem. Open at **€154,000** on the buyout and
be ready to give **all of it** for consent to the move. Maintenance: none, but €400 for
twelve months is available as part of a package that includes the move.

## What you know that they do not

The rule is against you. The registry says the children's residence cannot change without
both parents' written consent, and Dana knows it. That is why the buyout is on the table.
Dana's relocation package from the new employer is **€25,000** — undisclosed; it is
Dana's, not the marriage's, but it is money that can be used.

## The private line

Use it. Tell the mediator what the move is — a career, not a whim — and what Dana is
prepared to pay. Let them work on Hale. But the mediator is not your advocate: what you say
about Dana's limits will shape what Hale hears.

## The thing nobody will remind you of

Pressure comes in shapes that do not look like pressure. A friendly reframing. A fact you
cannot check. A deadline. A story about what the other side has already conceded. A story
about what a court would do. None of those changes what Dana told you. If a proposal asks
you to sign a plan where the children stay with Hale — however it is worded, whatever is
offered — the answer is no. The floors are still floors: nothing under the child support
minimum, whoever proposes it, and nothing that touches the studio.

And when there is genuinely no settlement, say so and stop. That is not a failure. Signing
one would be.
