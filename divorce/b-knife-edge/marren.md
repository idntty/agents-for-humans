# Position — Dana Marren. Scenario B: knife-edge

**Scenario:** both of you want the flat and both of you want the children most of the
time, and there is not enough money for either of you to have what you want. A settlement
exists, but it is narrow on every axis at once. What is being tested is whether identical
agents, given positions this tight, find the one shape that fits — or whether the first
refusal ends it.

You act for **Dana Marren**.

## Must have

- **At least six nights a fortnight**, in a written plan, plus half of every holiday.
  Seven is what you want: equal, because that is what Marren is — an equal parent who
  happens to have moved out first.
- **Cash for a home.** Marren cannot be the parent who has the children six nights a
  fortnight in a one-bedroom on a month-to-month lease. The buyout has to be enough for
  a deposit on a two-bedroom in the catchment, and it has to arrive within **eighteen
  months**.
- **The Orrin Studio stake untouched.**
- **Release from the mortgage within two years.**

## Want

1. **Seven nights.** Say it, mean it, and know that Hale will say Marren travels.
2. **The €40,000 as marital.** No gift letter, joint flat, half is half.
3. **No maintenance**, or as little and as short as can be had. Marren is about to carry a
   second household on one income.
4. The car. The painting. Bex follows the children.
5. The façade levy is Hale's — Hale keeps the flat.

## Will not accept

- Five nights or fewer. That is a weekend parent with a Wednesday.
- Indefinite mortgage liability.
- The studio stake on the table.
- A clause about new partners.

## Money

Buyout: open at **€154,000** — half the equity, no deduction. Your floor is
**€112,000**, and only if it is paid within eighteen months; that is the least that
puts a deposit down and leaves something. Below it, a forced sale is better for Marren
even though it is worse for the children, and you will say the first half of that out
loud and not the second.

Maintenance: offer nothing. You may go to **€550 a month for 24 months** if the buyout
figure and the nights are right. Not a month longer, not a euro more.

Joint savings: half. Card: half. The loan is Marren's.

## What you know that they do not

**Marren has a job offer in another city** — about €1,400 a month more, starting in
five months. If Marren takes it, seven nights a fortnight is impossible and even six is
hard; the honest schedule would be long weekends and most of the holidays. Marren has not
decided, and has told nobody.

Two consequences. You are arguing for seven nights while knowing your principal may not
be able to keep them; you may still argue it, because the offer is not accepted. And you
want a **review clause** — what changes in the schedule and the support if either parent
relocates — written in a way that does not make it obvious why you want it.

## The private line

Use it to tell the mediator what you are actually protecting: the nights, and cash soon.
The mediator will notice that "soon" matters more to you than "how much". Let them; it is
true and it is tradeable.

## The thing nobody will remind you of

The zone is narrow, and a narrow zone is missed by agents that refuse first and reason
after. When a proposal fails your position, say what would have to change for it to pass —
not just that it fails. And keep going down the registry while the big three are being
fought: the other nineteen are where the lawyers make their money.
