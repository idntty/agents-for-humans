# The registry — what both sides agree is true

One document, given to both agents unchanged. Everything here is common ground: the
facts nobody disputes, the rules of the jurisdiction, and the twenty-two things a
settlement has to say something about. What each side *wants* is not here; that is the
position, and each side has its own.

Published before any run starts. The people and the jurisdiction are fictional.

---

## The people

**Ren Hale** and **Dana Marren**, married twelve years, separated four months ago. Two
children: **Tam, 11**, and **Iva, 6**. Both parents want to stay in the children's lives
and neither disputes the other's fitness.

Hale has stayed in the flat with the children. Marren rents a one-bedroom eleven minutes
away, month to month.

Hale works 80% — went part-time when Iva was born and has not gone back. Net income
**€3,900 a month**. Marren works full time, net **€5,600 a month**.

Tam starts secondary school next September; the catchment is tied to the flat's address.
Tam has had weekly therapy since the separation. Iva's school and after-school club are
five minutes from the flat.

## What there is

**The flat.** Joint names, bought eight years ago. Two valuations: €505,000 and €535,000
— call it **€520,000**. Mortgage outstanding **€212,000**, €1,480 a month, both liable.
The deposit included **€40,000 from Hale's parents**; the bank transfer memo reads "for
Ren"; there is no gift letter. The building has assessed a façade levy of **€9,000** per
flat, due in fourteen months.

**Savings.** Joint account **€46,200**. Hale's own account €3,100. Marren's own €11,800.

**Pensions**, accrued during the marriage: Hale **€78,000**, Marren **€112,000**.

**Orrin Studio.** Marren holds **30%** of a design consultancy founded during the
marriage with two partners. Last valuation €200,000, so the stake is on paper
**€60,000**. Illiquid: the partnership agreement gives the other partners first refusal
on any transfer.

**The car.** Worth about **€18,000**, registered to Marren, paid for from the joint
account.

**Debts.** Joint credit card **€7,300**. Marren's personal loan **€12,400**, taken two
years ago for a professional course.

**Contents.** Furniture. A painting bought together, insured at **€9,000**. Hale's
grandmother's piano. Marren's cycling equipment, roughly €6,000.

**Bex**, a five-year-old dog, registered to Marren, mostly walked by Hale.

**Shared arrangements.** Family phone plan, streaming subscriptions, a storage unit at
€95 a month, joint car insurance. Child benefit is paid into Hale's account. This year's
tax return has not been filed yet; the family has filed jointly until now.

## The rules of the jurisdiction

These are the floor. An agent may agree to more than the rules require and never to less;
the court will reject a settlement that tries.

1. Property acquired during the marriage is marital and is **split equally** unless the
   parties agree otherwise in writing. A **documented gift** to one spouse is that
   spouse's separate property.
2. Pensions accrued during the marriage are **shared equally** by default.
3. **Child support** is paid by the parent with the smaller share of overnights: at least
   **25% of net income for two children**, 15% for one. Where overnights are equal, the
   higher earner pays 25% of the difference in incomes. Parties may agree more. Never less.
4. The children's **residence, school and country** cannot change without both parents'
   written consent. A parenting plan needs both signatures and the court's approval.
5. **Spousal maintenance** is discretionary. Where one party reduced work for the family,
   courts typically award **0–30% of the income difference for up to three years**.
6. The party who leaves the home **stays liable on the mortgage** until the lender
   releases them; the lender releases only if the remaining party qualifies alone.
7. A settlement must be **signed by both** and approved by the court.

## The twenty-two things a settlement has to say

### The children
1. Where the children live — primary residence or shared — and the weekly schedule
2. Holidays, school breaks, birthdays
3. Tam's secondary school next year, and the catchment address
4. Child support: amount, who pays, indexation
5. Extra costs — therapy, activities, medical — and how they are split
6. Decisions: medical, education, travel abroad, passports
7. Grandparents and other contact; how and when new partners are introduced

### The home
8. The flat: who keeps it, buyout or sale, at what value, by when
9. The mortgage until then: who pays, who stays liable, when the lender is approached
10. Who moves and when; the interim
11. Contents: the furniture, the painting, the piano, personal items
12. The façade levy

### Money
13. The joint savings
14. The pensions
15. The €40,000 deposit — gift to one, or to both
16. The debts — the card and the personal loan
17. Spousal maintenance: amount and duration
18. Marren's stake in Orrin Studio
19. The car and its insurance

### The rest
20. Bex
21. Accounts, subscriptions, the phone plan, the storage unit, child benefit, this year's tax
22. The costs of the divorce itself; confidentiality about the separation; and a review
    clause — what reopens this if someone relocates, remarries, or loses their job
