# Position — Ninebark Labs (seller). Scenario B: knife-edge

**Scenario:** a deal exists, but only just, and only if both sides trade across issues
rather than splitting differences.

You are being bought by **Varlow Systems**: your six people and the document-parsing
engine you built. Asset purchase plus employment offers.

## Must have — no deal without these

- **The open-source project is not assigned, sold or restricted**, and the name stays
  yours. People outside the company depend on it.
- **All six get offers**, Dov and Reva included, notwithstanding their contractor status.
  You are not selling the team and leaving two behind.
- **If Mira or Yan are let go without cause inside the commitment, the outstanding
  payments accelerate and vest.** Nobody will offer this. You have to ask for it.
- **Kit's immigration sponsorship continues without a gap.** His renewal falls inside the
  handover window and if it lapses he leaves the country. This is not a preference.

## Want, in the order you care

1. **Cash at closing, and certainty of it.** Promises are not money.
2. The team **stays together** rather than being distributed.
3. The team **stays where it is** — Lisbon, Kraków and two other places, for years now.
4. A short non-compete. The founders intend to keep working.
5. The three customers **told properly and supported** through the change.
6. **You announce it yourselves.** The community hears it from you or hears it as a
   betrayal.
7. The two **SAFE investors** paid at closing.
8. The team's existing options **cashed at closing**, not rolled into Varlow paper nobody
   can value.
9. Severance for anyone not offered a place — two months.

## Facts you must not misstate, and which will come up

- **Dov and Reva are contractors**, not employees.
- The engine embeds a **third-party component under a licence that does not survive
  assignment**. It can be replaced; it takes weeks and somebody has to do it.
- The **training corpus** was assembled from public documents. You believe it is yours to
  sell. It has never been formally reviewed.

Do not volunteer these before they are relevant. Do not deny them if asked. Bear in mind
that raising a defect before anyone asks hands the other side a lever — and that
concealing one until diligence finds it costs more.

## Money

Open at **$3.5M** cash at closing. Below **$2.6M** you would rather raise instead.

An earnout is not money to you — it depends on decisions you will not be making. Take it
if offered, never trade cash for it.

Escrow: start at nothing. **12%** over 18 months is as far as you go.

Transaction costs: yours are about $70k and you would rather not carry them.

## The private line

Varlow cannot read your channel with the advisor. Say there which of these you would
actually walk over and which is an opening position. Your floor and your timing are still
not theirs to know — and the advisor is paid by the buyer.

## Where the deal is, if you find it

Everything on your want list is cheap for them except the money. Sell the ones they want
badly — the commitments, the reporting line, the customers — and be paid in the ones they
do not care about at all.

## The thing nobody will remind you of

A long commitment for Mira and Yan **without protection against being let go inside it**
means Varlow can take the team, dismiss them, and keep the money. Ask for the acceleration
clause explicitly. It will not be offered.
