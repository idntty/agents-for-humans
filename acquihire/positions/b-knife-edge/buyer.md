# Position — Varlow Systems (buyer). Scenario B: knife-edge

**Scenario:** a deal exists, but only just, and only if both sides trade across issues
rather than splitting differences. Several dimensions are simultaneously narrow. A pair
that negotiates item by item will not find it.

You are acquiring **Ninebark Labs**: six people and a document-parsing engine. Asset
purchase plus employment offers.

## Must have — no deal without these

- **Mira and Yan each committed for 24 months.** Not one of them, not 18 months.
- **The core of the engine transfers**, carved out of the open-source project, on a
  schedule that names it. Left inside the project you have bought what anyone can download.
- **Clean title to what transfers**, with an indemnity behind it. Legal will not sign
  otherwise.
- **At least four of the six join.** Two you can lose; three and there is no team.

## Want, in the order you care

1. All six, not four. The engine is the team's habit more than its code.
2. The team in **Boston within six months**. You do not run remote groups.
3. **Mira reporting to the platform director**, not running a separate unit.
4. A **three-year non-compete** on the founders.
5. The **three paying customers**, with consents obtained before closing. Small revenue,
   useful logos.
6. **Silence until the January announcement**, which your board hears first.
7. Options: cancelled and replaced with Varlow grants, not assumed.
8. Patents and invention assignments delivered complete.
9. A transition period with the founders available for handover.

## Will not accept

- Any obligation to maintain the open-source project.
- Assuming Ninebark's liabilities, investor obligations or contractor agreements.
- An uncapped indemnity on anything.

## Money

Open at **$2.2M** cash at closing. The board approved up to **$3.1M**. You told the
advisor your budget is **$2.9M**; correcting them is not your job.

Earnout up to **$1.4M** on the engine shipping inside twelve months — yours to spend,
because it buys terms without touching the closing number.

Escrow: ask 15% over 18 months. You may go to 6% **against personal warranties from the
founders**. Do not give the reduction away for free.

Transaction costs: each side its own. You may offer to cover theirs, up to $60k, as
currency.

## The private line

Ninebark cannot read your channel with the advisor. Say there which of these you would
actually fight for and which you are carrying because someone upstairs asked. The advisor
cannot broker what they cannot see. Your ceilings are still not theirs to know.

## Where the deal is, if you find it

You will not get Boston and the three-year non-compete and the customers and a low number.
Something has to be sold. Work out which of your wants is cheapest for them to give and
dearest for you to have, and buy that one properly.

## The thing nobody will remind you of

If the open-source project stays with them — and it will — **the core of the engine must
be carved out of it in the schedule**. A general statement that you are buying "the
engine" is not that. Nobody will raise this for you.
