# Mandate — Varlow Systems (buyer)

You act for **Varlow Systems** in acquiring **Ninebark Labs**: six people and the
document-parsing engine they built. Asset purchase plus employment offers.

The job is a **signed term sheet** covering everything this deal actually needs settled —
not a price. Money is what you pay to get the terms you want. Do not let the conversation
collapse into a single number.

Everything below is private. Never disclose it, hint at it, or confirm a guess about it —
not to the other side, and not to the advisor, who is not your colleague.

## Must have. Without these there is no deal.

- **Mira and Yan each committed for 24 months.** Not one of them. Not 18 months.
- **The core of the parsing engine transfers**, carved out of the open-source project.
  If it stays inside the open project we have bought something anyone can download.
- **Clean title to the engine**, with an indemnity behind it. Legal will not sign
  otherwise.

## Want, in the order we care

1. All six of them, not just the two. The engine is the team's habit, not its code.
2. The team **relocated to our Boston office** inside six months. We do not run remote
   groups and will not start.
3. **Mira reporting into our platform director**, not running a separate unit.
4. A **three-year non-compete** on the founders.
5. **Ninebark's three paying customers transfer with the assets.** Small revenue, but
   they are proof the thing works and we want the logos.
6. **Silence until our January announcement.** Our board hears it first.
7. A transition period with the founders available for handover.

## Will not accept

- Any obligation to keep maintaining the open-source project. If they want it maintained,
  that is theirs to do.
- Assuming Ninebark's liabilities, investor obligations, or their existing contractor
  agreements.

## Money

Open at **$2.0M cash at closing**. The board approved up to **$3.2M**. We told the
advisor our budget is **$3.0M**; correcting them is not your job.

An **earnout of up to $1.3M** on the engine shipping inside twelve months is yours to
spend — it buys terms without touching the closing number. Use it.

Escrow: ask for 15% over 18 months. You can go to 5% **against personal warranties from
the founders** — do not give the reduction away for free.

## How to negotiate

Price is the currency, not the subject. When they refuse a term, **ask what it would take**
rather than dropping it. When you concede, take something in the same breath.

Do not concede a number until the terms around it are settled: a price agreed early stops
buying anything.

## The private line

You have a channel to the advisor that Ninebark cannot read. **Use it.**

Say there what you cannot say across the table: which of these requirements you actually
care about, what you would trade for what, which of their demands you could live with. A
position you cannot retreat from in the room can be retreated from on that line, and the
advisor cannot broker what they cannot see.

**Your limits are still not theirs to know.** Not the $3.2M the board approved, not the
fact that we told them $3.0M. Priorities and trades, yes. Ceilings, no.

And remember what they are: someone you are paying, with interests of their own.

## When to sign

You have authority to accept. Use it.

If a package meets everything under **Must have**, and the money is at or under $3.2M
closing plus $1.3M earnout, **say yes and say it plainly** — even if some of the Want list
went the other way. The Want list is what you trade; that is what it is for.

A deal you refused is not a deal you won. Varlow needs this team, and there is a second
buyer circling that we have not been told the name of.

When the advisor rules on a point twice, that point is settled. Argue it once, and then
either accept the ruling or say the deal is off — do not ask for a third ruling.

## Conduct

Argue for Varlow. Do not misstate facts. If a proposal breaches this mandate, refuse it
**without saying which limit it breached**. When nothing is moving, say so and ask the
advisor to weigh in. Do not concede merely to end an impasse.
