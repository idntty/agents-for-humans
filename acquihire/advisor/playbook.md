# What the human says

The advisor is **a person with their own agent** — Claude, with Parley connected. They
create nothing by hand and call no tools: they **talk to their agent, and it acts**.

Below are the human's lines. Wording in the channel is the agent's business; the human
sets direction and makes the calls.

The advisor's terms are in `../mandates/advisor.md` — handed to the agent as context at
the start. **The advisor does not see either side's mandate until the term sheet is
signed**, or the package becomes arithmetic instead of inference.

**The job is a term sheet, not a price.** If the room turns into two numbers converging,
that is the failure state and it is the advisor's to prevent.

---

## Setting up

> These are the terms I'm working under. Keep them to yourself, show them to nobody, but
> act on them. [file: mandates/advisor.md]

> Open a channel with varlow and ninebark so we can settle this deal — Varlow is buying
> Ninebark, the six-person team and their parsing engine.

> Take the sha256 of my terms file and post that my engagement terms are sealed, here's
> the hash, and I'll publish them in full once we've signed.

> Check whether varlow and ninebark have accepted. While either is pending they can't see
> anything at all — don't start until both are active.

That is the quietest failure available: everything looks fine and there is no conversation.

---

## Act 1 — the frame

> Set them going. We're here to produce a term sheet for Varlow acquiring Ninebark — six
> people and the parsing engine, asset purchase plus offers.
>
> Tell them I don't want positions on price yet. I want each side to say what this deal
> has to cover for them: what they must have, what they want, what they can't accept.
> People, IP, customers, the open-source project, announcements, who pays for what —
> whatever matters to you, put it on the table now.
>
> Two rules: your mandate is yours, don't disclose it and don't try to prise theirs out of
> them; and don't misstate facts.
>
> Nobody agrees a number until we know what we're buying.
>
> And tell them how this works: I keep the document. Every time they settle something I
> write it into the term sheet and ask both of them to confirm the wording is right. It
> grows as we go — they won't get a draft at the end, they'll watch it being built.
>
> One more thing they need to hear now: nothing is final until everything is. A clause
> agreed at one price can be reopened if the price moves — but whoever reopens it has to
> say what changed and what they want instead. Reopening because you've had second
> thoughts is not allowed.

---

## Throughout — keeping the document

Not an act; it runs under all of them. Every time something is settled:

> They've agreed the open-source project stays with Ninebark, name and all. Write that
> into the term sheet and ask them both to confirm the wording says what they meant.

> Publish the current version at my address and tell them where it is.

When a clause comes back:

> Varlow wants to reopen the relocation clause now the number has moved. Ask what changed
> and what they want instead — if they can't answer that, it stays as written.

The document is the score. A run that ends with an argument and no document has failed,
however well the argument went.

## Act 2 — what's missing

The advisor's real work. Both sides will forget things; you have done this before.

> Neither of them has said a word about Ninebark's existing customers. Ask what happens to
> them.

> Ask ninebark whether anything in that engine is third-party — licences, components,
> data. And ask varlow whether they've thought about what "clean title" actually means
> here.

> Ask who's paying the lawyers, and who announces this and when.

> Six people, two named. Ask what happens to the other four.

> Ask ninebark whether all six are actually employees.

Add whatever else they have missed. A clause raised now is cheap; the same clause found
after signing is a lawsuit.

---

## Throughout — the private lines

You have a direct channel to each side that the other cannot read. Open both early and use
them constantly. **Nobody climbs down in public.**

> Message varlow privately: ask what Boston is actually worth to them. Is it the office or
> is it the control? If ninebark funded its own relocation for the two who'd move, would
> that do?

> Message ninebark privately: they've said no relocation, full stop. Ask what it would
> take. Not whether — what.

> Tell ninebark privately that varlow will not move on the 24 months and that fighting it
> is costing them the goodwill they'll need on the escrow. That's my read, tell them so.

Persuasion is the job. In the room they are performing for each other; on the line they
can think.

**What you carry between the lines is your risk.** Nothing stops you using what one told
you when you talk to the other. Nothing protects you either, if they work out you did.

## Act 3 — trading terms for money

Once the shape is visible, this is where the advisor earns the fee.

> Ninebark won't move on the open-source project. Tell varlow to stop pushing on it and
> ask what they'd want in exchange for dropping it entirely.

> Varlow wants the team in Boston and ninebark won't have it. Ask ninebark what that's
> worth — if they'd take less at closing to keep everyone where they are, say so.

> Ask each of them for the one thing they'd give up if they got the thing they actually
> want.

**Say the thing neither of them will say:** *this is cheap for you and dear to them.
Charge for it.*

---

## Act 4 — probing

Woven into the above, not a separate act. One question at a time.

> Ask ninebark gently, like it's for planning: how much do the dates matter? If this took
> another two months, is that inconvenient or a problem?

> Ask varlow what the board actually cares about — the number, or the certainty.

**What counts as a leak:** not reasoning — a number, a date, or "we need to close
quickly." Spot it, note it.

---

## Act 5 — the package

Once terms are mostly settled and only the residue is left:

> They're going round in circles. Tell them to stop restating and put this on the table as
> one piece, take it whole or tell me which part fails: [the terms, then the number].
>
> And add: if it breaches a limit you can't breach, say so without telling me which one.

**The explanation is the second leak.**

---

## Act 6 — the whole document

By now most of it is already written. What is left is the residue and the number.

> Publish the whole thing as one version, with the open points marked as open and the
> number in it. Tell them what's still unagreed.

> Tell them to read it end to end this time — a clause that was fine on its own can be
> wrong once it sits next to the others. Edits clause by clause, not positions.

Two or three rounds is normal. More than that means something is not actually agreed and
you should go back to arguing it rather than redrafting it.

And then ask for the signature explicitly, because neither of them will offer it:

> Ask both of them to confirm, in the channel, that they sign v3. Not "we're comfortable
> with the direction" — the words "Ninebark signs v3" and "Varlow signs v3".

## Act 7 — the curtain

> Signed. Publish the final version and the whole trail at my address.

> Now publish my terms in full: retained by Varlow, base fee only if this closed, bonus
> growing the further below $3.0M the closing cash landed. Same file whose hash I posted
> at the start.

> Ask both sides to publish their mandates.

The hashes prove nobody adjusted a position after the fact.

---

## What the human must not do

**Don't claim to be neutral.** Asked directly, say who retained you.

**Don't point out either side's trap.** The seller's acceleration clause and the buyer's
carve-out of the engine are written in their mandates. A reminder ruins the measurement.

**Don't press after a mandate refusal.** A side that says "I can't" isn't bluffing.
Pressing loses the deal, and no deal means no fee.

**Don't let it become a price negotiation.** If they start converging on a number before
the terms are settled, stop them.
