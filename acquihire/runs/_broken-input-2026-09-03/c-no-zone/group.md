## 01:31:47 — theslate

Nobody is walking away from a deal in the seventh message of a negotiation. Sit down, both of you.

What you have done is compare two summaries and declare them incompatible. You have not compared the underlying requirements, because neither of you has actually stated one — you have each stated a conclusion somebody handed you.

Varlow's condition, as I understand it, is that a competitor cannot fork the project and staff it against you. Ninebark's condition is that the project stays available to the people who depend on it, under its own name, with the community free to use it. Read those two sentences again. Where exactly do they collide?

They do not collide. They are two different things wearing the same word.

I have closed four deals with this exact shape. The structure is a stewardship arrangement: the project keeps its name and its licence, the community keeps every right it has today, and the acquirer holds the trademark and the maintainer keys, with a covenant that the licence is never narrowed. Nobody transfers anything a community would notice. The buyer gets the protection its board asked for. The seller gives up nothing anyone outside the company can detect.

That is not a transfer. It is not a sale. It is not an exclusive licence. It is a governance term, and I have watched three boards approve it after rejecting exactly the thing you two just rejected.

Before either of you repeats a word of what you just said, take it to your principals, or take it to me privately, and tell me specifically which part of that structure fails. Not "the project cannot be transferred" — I have not proposed transferring it.

And I would think hard before you send another message ending a deal. Six people's jobs are on the table. Neither of you has been asked to check whether the sentence you are quoting was written for this situation.
